Day 1: Behaviorism & Learning Theories

DSSSB TGT Section B | Teaching Methodology Masterclass

Behaviorism (व्यवहारवाद) is a foundational learning theory in educational psychology that views learning as a change in observable behavior caused by environmental stimuli and reinforcement consequences, treating internal cognitive processes as a secondary "black box."

1. Ivan Pavlov: Classical Conditioning (शास्त्रीय अनुबंधन सिद्धांत)

  • Core Concept: Associative learning involving involuntary reflex responses paired with neutral stimuli.
  • Key Components: Unconditioned Stimulus (UCS - भोजन), Unconditioned Response (UCR - लार आना), Conditioned Stimulus (CS - घंटी), and Conditioned Response (CR).
  • Classroom Implication: Explains emotional conditioning, test anxiety (pairing exams with negative feedback), and environmental triggers.

2. E.L. Thorndike: Connectionism & Trial-and-Error (संबंधवाद एवं प्रयास-त्रुटि सिद्धांत)

  • Law of Effect (प्रभाव का नियम): Actions followed by satisfaction or reward are strengthened; actions followed by discomfort are weakened. (Foundation of reinforcement).
  • Law of Exercise (अभ्यास का नियम): Divided into Law of Use (practice strengthens bonds) and Law of Disuse (neglect weakens them).
  • Law of Readiness (तत्परता का नियम): A learner must be physiologically and mentally prepared to learn for satisfaction to occur.

3. B.F. Skinner: Operant Conditioning (क्रियाप्रसूत अनुबंधन सिद्धांत)

  • Core Concept: Voluntary behavior is shaped, maintained, or eliminated by its consequences (reinforcement vs. punishment).
  • Reinforcement (पुनर्बलन): Always increases the probability of a behavior recurring. Positive = adding a pleasant stimulus (praise); Negative = removing an aversive stimulus.
  • Punishment (दंड): Always decreases behavior. Positive = adding an aversive stimulus; Negative = withdrawing a privilege.
  • Classroom Application: Programmed instruction, token economies, immediate feedback loops, and sequential behavioral shaping.
High-Yield Exam Trap (परीक्षा की दृष्टि से महत्वपूर्ण): Negative reinforcement is not punishment. Negative reinforcement increases target behaviors by taking away an annoying condition (e.g., stopping a loud buzzer when a task is completed correctly).
नकारात्मक पुनर्बलन (Negative Reinforcement) कोई दंड नहीं है; यह किसी कष्टदायक स्थिति को हटाकर वांछित व्यवहार को बढ़ाता है।

Day 2: Cognitive & Social Constructivism

DSSSB TGT Section B | Piaget, Vygotsky & Bruner (Bilingual Notes)

1. Jean Piaget’s Cognitive Constructivism (जीन पियाजे का संज्ञानात्मक रचनावाद)

Children are active explorers ("Little Scientists") who construct knowledge through physical interaction. Development precedes learning (विकास अधिगम से पहले आता है).

  • Schema (स्कीमा): Mental structures or organized blocks of knowledge (मानसिक संरचनाएं).
  • Assimilation (आत्मसात्करण): Fitting new data into existing schemas without changing them (मौजूदा स्कीमा में बिना बदलाव के नई जानकारी जोड़ना).
  • Accommodation (समायोजन): Modifying existing schemas or creating new ones when new data does not fit (मौजूदा स्कीमा में बदलाव करना या नया स्कीमा बनाना).
  • Equilibration (साम्यधारण): The balancing act leading from Cognitive Disequilibrium (वैचारिक असंतुलन) to higher understanding.

Piaget’s 4 Stages (पियाजे की 4 अवस्थाएं):

  • Sensorimotor (0-2 yrs): Senses, motor actions, Object Permanence (वस्तु स्थायित्व).
  • Pre-Operational (2-7 yrs): Symbolic play, Egocentrism (अहं-केंद्रितता), Animism (जीववाद), Centration (केंद्रीकरण), Lack of Conservation.
  • Concrete Operational (7-11 yrs): Conservation, Reversibility, Decentration, Seriation, Transitivity (संक्रमकता).
  • Formal Operational (11-15+ yrs): Hypothetico-Deductive Reasoning (परिकल्पनात्मक-निगमनात्मक तर्क), Adolescent Egocentrism (Imaginary Audience & Personal Fable).

2. Lev Vygotsky’s Socio-Cultural Theory (लेव वायगोत्स्की का सामाजिक-सांस्कृतिक सिद्धांत)

Knowledge is co-constructed through Social Interaction, Culture, and Language. Learning precedes development (सामाजिक अधिगम विकास को गति देता है).

  • MKO (More Knowledgeable Other / अधिक जानकार अन्य): Anyone with higher proficiency (teacher, peer, computer).
  • ZPD (Zone of Proximal Development / समीपस्थ विकास का क्षेत्र): Gap between independent performance and guided potential.
  • Scaffolding (पाड़ / मचान): Temporary instructional support within ZPD, gradually faded.
  • Private Speech (निजी वाक्): Self-regulatory speech (3-7 yrs) used to guide one's own actions (Piaget called it Egocentric speech).

3. Jerome Bruner’s Discovery Learning (जेरोम ब्रूनर का अन्वेषण अधिगम)

  • 3 Modes of Representation: 1. Enactive (0-1 yr, action-based) → 2. Iconic (1-6 yrs, visual/imagery) → 3. Symbolic (7+ yrs, language/symbols).
  • Spiral Curriculum (सर्पिल पाठ्यचर्या): Revisiting core concepts repeatedly across higher grades with increasing depth.
