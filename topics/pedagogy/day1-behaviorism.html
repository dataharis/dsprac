Day 1: Behaviorism & Learning Theories (व्यवहारवाद एवं अधिगम सिद्धांत)

DSSSB TGT Section B | Detailed Bilingual Study Notes

Behaviorism (व्यवहारवाद) is a systematic approach to understanding learning that focuses exclusively on observable behaviors while discounting mental activities (treating the mind as a "black box"). Learning is defined as a relatively permanent change in behavior resulting from environmental stimulus-response (S-R) conditioning.

1. Ivan Pavlov: Classical Conditioning (इवान पावलोव का शास्त्रीय अनुबंधन सिद्धांत)

Pavlov demonstrated that animals and humans can learn to associate a neutral stimulus with an unconditioned reflex response.

  • Unconditioned Stimulus (UCS / अस्वाभाविक उद्दीपक): Food that naturally triggers salivation (e.g., भोजन).
  • Unconditioned Response (UCR / अस्वाभाविक प्रतिक्रिया): Natural reflex of salivation to food (लार आना).
  • Conditioned Stimulus (CS / स्वाभाविक उद्दीपक): Initially neutral bell tone paired with food (घंटी).
  • Conditioned Response (CR / स्वाभाविक प्रतिक्रिया): Salivation triggered by the bell alone after conditioning.

2. E.L. Thorndike: Connectionism & Trial-and-Error (थार्नडाइक का संबंधवाद एवं प्रयास-त्रुटि सिद्धांत)

Thorndike formulated his theory using puzzle boxes with cats and established three primary laws of learning:

  • Law of Effect (प्रभाव का नियम): Responses followed by satisfaction/reward are strengthened; responses followed by discomfort are weakened. (Foundation of reinforcement).
  • Law of Exercise (अभ्यास का नियम): Divided into Law of Use (practice strengthens S-R bonds) and Law of Disuse (lack of practice weakens bonds).
  • Law of Readiness (तत्परता का नियम): A learner must be psychologically and physiologically prepared to learn for satisfaction to occur.

3. B.F. Skinner: Operant Conditioning (बी.एफ. स्किनर का क्रियाप्रसूत अनुबंधन सिद्धांत)

Skinner distinguished between respondent behavior (reflexes) and operant behavior (voluntary actions shaped by consequences).

  • Positive Reinforcement (धनात्मक पुनर्बलन): Adding a pleasant stimulus to increase behavior (e.g., praising a student).
  • Negative Reinforcement (ऋणात्मक पुनर्बलन): Removing an aversive stimulus to increase behavior (e.g., stopping a loud buzzer when a task is completed). Note: Negative reinforcement is NOT punishment.
  • Positive Punishment (धनात्मक दंड): Adding an aversive stimulus to decrease behavior (e.g., scolding).
  • Negative Punishment (ऋणात्मक दंड): Removing a pleasant privilege to decrease behavior (e.g., taking away recess time).
  • Shaping (आकार देना): Successive approximation toward a complex target behavior.
High-Yield DSSSB Exam Summary (मुख्य परीक्षा बिंदु):
• Pavlov: Classical Conditioning; Stimulus-Response (S-R); Involuntary reflexes.
• Thorndike: Trial and Error; Law of Effect, Exercise, Readiness.
• Skinner: Operant Conditioning; Reinforcement & Punishment; Programmed instruction.

Q1. Who is known as the pioneer of Classical Conditioning, and what was his primary experiment?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Pavlov established classical conditioning by pairing an unconditioned stimulus (food) with a neutral stimulus (bell) until the bell alone triggered salivation.

Q2. Thorndike’s "Law of Effect" states that:

Explanation (व्याख्या): The Law of Effect (प्रभाव का नियम) is the foundation of reinforcement theory. Actions that result in a satisfying outcome tend to be repeated.

Q3. Which of the following best describes "Operant Conditioning" as proposed by B.F. Skinner?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Operant conditioning (क्रियाप्रसूत अनुबंधन) focuses on how voluntary behaviors are modified by the consequences (rewards or punishments) that follow them.

Q4. In Thorndike’s trial-and-error theory of learning, the "Law of Exercise" is further divided into two parts. What are they?

Explanation (व्याख्या): The Law of Exercise (अभ्यास का नियम) states that practice strengthens connections (Law of Use) and lack of practice weakens them (Law of Disuse).

Q5. A teacher praises a student every time they complete their classwork on time, causing the student to finish work promptly every day. According to behaviorism, this praise acts as a:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Adding a desirable stimulus (praise) after a behavior to increase its frequency is called Positive Reinforcement.

Q6. Removing an unpleasant stimulus when a desired behavior is exhibited is known as:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Negative reinforcement (ऋणात्मक पुनर्बलन) increases a desired behavior by taking away an aversive (unpleasant) stimulus. It is NOT punishment.

Q7. Which learning law proposed by Thorndike emphasizes that a learner must be mentally and physically prepared to learn in order to find satisfaction in it?

Explanation (व्याख्या): The Law of Readiness (तत्परता का नियम) states that learning is satisfying when the organism is neurologically and psychologically ready to act.

Q8. Programmed instruction and teaching machines, which break learning down into small sequential steps with immediate feedback, are direct applications of whose theory?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Skinner's operant conditioning principles form the basis of programmed learning (प्रोग्राम्ड इंस्ट्रक्शन), where learning is divided into small, reinforced steps.

Q9. Which of the following represents a limitation of strict Behaviorism in a modern classroom setting?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Behaviorism treats the mind as a "black box" and ignores internal cognition, insight, and logical reasoning, which are essential for higher-order learning.

Q10. A student who was once scolded by a strict math teacher starts feeling anxious whenever they enter the math block or hear the school bell ring for math period. This is an example of:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Emotional responses (like anxiety or fear) triggered by environmental cues (like the math block) are classic examples of Pavlovian Classical Conditioning.

Q11. In Pavlov’s experiment, the unconditioned stimulus (UCS) was:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Food naturally and unconditionally triggers the dog to salivate, making it the Unconditioned Stimulus (UCS).

Q12. The gradual strengthening of a target behavior by reinforcing successive approximations is known as:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Shaping (आकार देना) is a Skinnerian technique where rewards are given for steps that progressively get closer to the desired complex behavior.

Q13. According to Thorndike, when an S-R bond is practiced frequently, the connection is strengthened. This refers to:

Explanation (व्याख्या): The 'Law of Use' (a sub-law of Exercise) posits that repeated practice strengthens the stimulus-response connection.

Q14. What happens during 'Extinction' (विलोपन / लोप) in Classical Conditioning?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Extinction occurs when the Conditioned Stimulus (e.g., bell) is rung continuously without presenting the Unconditioned Stimulus (food), causing the salivation to stop.

Q15. Which of the following psychologists is associated with the 'Puzzle Box' experiment?

Explanation (व्याख्या): E.L. Thorndike used a Puzzle Box to study how hungry cats learned to escape to get food, establishing the Trial-and-Error learning theory.

Q16. Taking away a student's favorite video game console because of disruptive behavior in class is an example of:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Removing a pleasant stimulus (the video game) to decrease an unwanted behavior (disruption) is defined as Negative Punishment (ऋणात्मक दंड).

Q17. The re-emergence of a previously extinguished conditioned response after a time delay is known as:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Spontaneous Recovery (स्वतः पुनःप्राप्ति) is when an extinguished reflex temporarily reappears after a rest period.

Q18. A child learns to fear a white rat and subsequently shows fear of white rabbits and cotton balls. This phenomenon in behaviorism is called:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Stimulus Generalization occurs when stimuli similar to the conditioned stimulus (like white, fluffy things) evoke the same conditioned response (fear).

Q19. Who among the following is considered the father of Radical Behaviorism?

Explanation (व्याख्या): B.F. Skinner is the founder of Radical Behaviorism, emphasizing that all behaviors, including language, are operantly conditioned.

Q20. In Thorndike’s theory, what term refers to the strengthening of connection resulting from the learner's satisfaction?

Explanation (व्याख्या): The Law of Effect states that satisfaction strengthens S-R connections, while dissatisfaction weakens them.

Q21. Which schedule of reinforcement provides a reward after an unpredictable number of responses, making the behavior extremely resistant to extinction (e.g., slot machines)?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Variable Ratio (परिवर्ती अनुपात) yields the highest and most steady response rates because the reward is unpredictable.

Q22. According to Behaviorism, how is 'Learning' formally defined?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Behaviorists define learning strictly as a relatively permanent change in observable behavior resulting from practice or experience.

Q23. If a dog salivates only to the specific tone of a 500Hz bell and not to a 400Hz bell, this demonstrates:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Stimulus Discrimination is the ability to distinguish between the conditioned stimulus and other similar stimuli.

Q24. A student stops volunteering answers in class after being consistently ignored by the teacher. This behavior change is an example of:

Explanation (व्याख्या): Operant Extinction occurs when a previously reinforced behavior (answering) is no longer followed by the reinforcing consequence (teacher attention), causing the behavior to fade.

Q25. Which of the following classroom techniques directly emerged from Skinner’s Operant Conditioning principles?

Explanation (व्याख्या): Token economies (rewarding tokens for good behavior) and Programmed Instruction (step-by-step learning with instant feedback) are direct applications of Skinner's operant reinforcement.
